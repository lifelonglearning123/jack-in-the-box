Page 02 · The Idea

Brand essence.

Jack in the Box Training exists to unleash your creativity. It's a training company for micro businesses — owner-led teams of 1–9 people who get told they're too small for "proper" innovation work. We exist because they're wrong.

The Jack character on a spring isn't decoration. It's the brand argument. Trapped energy, released. The box is the comfort zone, the legacy training, the corporate frameworks that don't fit. The character is creative thinking, breaking out.

Everything we make should feel like that release: a little louder than corporate, a little more handmade than tech, always practical, never twee.

Page 03 · Identity

The Jack mark.

The Jack mark is a hand-drawn character on a coiled spring bursting from an open box — the literal embodiment of the brand idea. It is the property of Jack in the Box Training Co. and must be used as drawn — never redrawn, re-illustrated, or "modernised".

Anatomy

  1. 1 The head — wide eyes, round mouth (surprise / eureka).
  2. 2 The rays — radiating from the head (energy / idea).
  3. 3 The spring — released, loose, kinetic.
  4. 4 The box — open, perspective-cubed.
  5. 5 The motion ticks — small marks signalling movement.

Clear space & minimum size

Always keep a clear space around the mark equal to the height of the Jack's head (the round face). No copy, edges or other marks may enter that zone. Minimum reproduction sizes:

  • Print 18 mm tall
  • Web 48 px tall
  • Favicon 32 px square

Page 04 · Identity

Logo placement.

Approved backgrounds

  • Cream — preferred
  • White
  • Charcoal
  • Orange — use sparingly*

* On orange backgrounds the head's white fill provides separation, but contrast on the rays and spring is reduced. Prefer cream, white or charcoal for primary placements.

Do not

  • Don't stretch or squash. The proportions are fixed.

  • Don't recolour. The mark is only ever produced in brand red/orange.

  • Don't rotate. The Jack always springs upward.

  • Don't place on busy or low-contrast imagery without a clear-space backing.

  • Don't apply heavy drop-shadows, glows or 3D. The mark is flat.

  • Don't fade it as a watermark. The character must read fully.

Page 07 · Devices

Visual devices.

Five reusable graphic devices give every Jack in the Box artefact a family resemblance — even before you spot the logo.

01 · Pinking-shear zigzag

The signature edge. Alternating triangular teeth, ~22 px tall and ~36 px wide, used to divide any two brand colour blocks. Always horizontal, never angled or decorative.

JACK IN THE BOX TRAINING CO ·

02 · Vertical ribbon labels

Thin charcoal strip pinned to the left edge of a layout, with white uppercase Inter set vertical-rl. Used for section markers, archive numbers, recurring brand callouts.

03 · Halftone dot fields

Two-tone dot pattern overlaid at low opacity on flat colour blocks. Evokes mid-century commercial print and adds tactile depth without imagery.

Page 08 · Voice

Tone of voice.

Write the way Ian talks — direct, warm, a little dry. We sound like a senior who actually runs a business, not a consultancy deck.

  • 01

    Short sentences. Strong verbs.

    If a sentence runs over two lines, break it. Use active verbs ("we build", "we run") not "is provided" or "leverages".

  • 02

    British English. Plain words.

    "Organisation" not "Organization". "Workshop" not "experience". "Help" not "facilitate".

  • 03

    One idea per sentence.

    Especially in headlines. The display copy should read at a glance from across a room.

  • 04

    Confident, never boastful.

    "Hundreds of micro businesses use our tools" — not "Industry-leading transformation partner."

  • 05

    Specific over abstract.

    "1–9 people" beats "small teams". "Full-day workshop" beats "an immersive experience".

  • 06

    One emoji-free zone.

    No emoji in marketing copy or training materials. Use the brand's typographic ✱ or ► instead when you need a visual mark.
